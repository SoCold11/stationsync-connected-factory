## Improvement 1 — Correct Station Health Calculation

### Problem identified

During functional testing, Station A-104 displayed a green "All clear" status despite having an unexpected physical asset observed at that station.

The station-level health calculation appears to consider discrepancies involving assets officially assigned to a station but does not account for assets physically observed there that are assigned elsewhere.

This creates a misleading station health indicator.

### Required behavior

Update station health so it accounts for both:

- **Assigned-asset discrepancies:** Assets approved for the station that are not fully synchronized.
- **Unexpected physical assets:** Assets currently observed at the station whose approved assignment belongs to another station.

Rules:

1. Show "All clear" only when there are no assigned-asset discrepancies AND no unexpected physical assets at that station.
2. If either condition exists, display an attention status rather than a green "All clear" badge.
3. Show an accurate count or description of station-local exceptions. Avoid counting the same asset twice within one station.
4. Preserve the existing visual styling for healthy and unhealthy statuses.
5. Preserve the separate "unexpected" indicator on station cards.
6. Keep factory-wide KPI counts based on unique assets. Do not inflate the global "Require attention" count merely because one asset affects multiple stations.
7. Use existing domain evaluation and observation information rather than introducing a separate conflicting source of truth.

### Example

TT-1920 is approved for A-102 but physically observed at A-104.

Even if every officially assigned asset at A-104 is synchronized, A-104 should NOT display "All clear" because an unexpected tool remains at that station.

After the tool's reassignment is approved and all necessary digital records are reconciled, the exception should disappear and the station can return to "All clear."

### Suggested code location

Inspect `src/components/Overview.tsx`, particularly the logic calculating `issueCount`, `unexpected`, and the station status badge.

Update the existing calculation with the smallest appropriate change.

## Improvement 2 — Improve Supporting Text Readability

### Problem identified

Several areas of the StationSync interface have small, low-contrast supporting text that can be difficult to read during a live presentation on a large screen.

Examples include:

- Asset timestamps and last-updated labels.
- Physical observation evidence details.
- Equipment descriptions and serial numbers.
- KPI descriptions.
- Work-center descriptions and asset counts.
- Table column headers and secondary row descriptions.
- Activity History timestamps and metadata.
- Form help text, confirmation statements, and explanatory messages.
- Asset Inspector labels.
- Small instructional text near workflow buttons.

### Required changes

1. Increase the contrast of secondary and supporting text against its background.
2. Use darker, accessible slate-blue or neutral-gray text where appropriate.
3. Target WCAG AA contrast standards for readable normal text, ideally 4.5:1 or better.
4. Increase exceptionally small font sizes modestly where necessary, especially text around 9–10px.
5. Preserve the distinction between headings, primary content, secondary information, and less-important metadata.
6. Maintain the existing StationSync visual design, typography, spacing, card layouts, and color palette.
7. Do not modify the meaning of status colors or reduce the visibility of warnings and success indicators.
8. Preserve responsive behavior and avoid overflowing text, crowded layouts, or broken alignment.

### Suggested code location

Inspect `src/styles.css` and the existing typography/color classes.

Prefer targeted improvements to existing styles rather than introducing unnecessary new components or overriding the entire stylesheet.
